Is there a free trial?

Yes. The RuckTrack (KiezelPay) listing on the Connect IQ Store includes a 7-day free trial. You install it, use it for a week, then complete the purchase only if you want to keep it. After the trial, the app shows a code that you enter at www.kzl.io/code to finish the purchase. No payment info required up front.

The original RuckTrack listing (Garmin Pay, $2.99 USD) is the same app without the trial step. If you already know you want it, that's the simpler path.

For the full comparison between the two listings, see the next question.

What's the difference between RuckTrack and RuckTrack (KiezelPay)?

Same app, same price. The difference is the payment system and the trial.

  • RuckTrack uses Garmin Pay, the standard Connect IQ Store payment system. $2.99 USD, no trial, instant install after purchase. Store listing.
  • RuckTrack (KiezelPay) uses KiezelPay, a third-party payment system Garmin permits for Connect IQ apps. $2.99 USD with a 7-day free trial. Store listing.

They are the same app. Same 86 supported devices, same calorie math, same screens, same fields in Garmin Connect.

Pick KiezelPay if you want to use it for a week before paying, or if Garmin Pay isn't available where you live. Pick the Garmin Pay listing if you already know you want it and would rather buy once and be done.

If you're choosing fresh: the KiezelPay version lets you try the app for 7 days before committing.

What is rucking?

Rucking is walking with a weighted pack. The load adds resistance to an otherwise low-impact activity, increasing calorie burn and engaging your posterior chain and core. It started as a military fitness staple, called tabbing or yomping in the British forces, and has become mainstream in hiking, strength training, and endurance circles.

Which Garmin watches does RuckTrack work on?

RuckTrack runs on 86 Garmin devices across AMOLED and MIP displays, including Forerunner (70 / 165 / 170 / 255 / 265 / 570 / 955 / 965 / 970), fēnix 7 / 8 / E, Enduro 3, Venu (2 / 3 / 4 / Sq 2 / X1), epix Pro, Instinct 3 AMOLED, D2 Mach / Air, Descent MK3 / G2, Approach S50 / S70, MARQ 2, tactix 7 / 8, quatix, and vívoactive 5 / 6. See the full devices page for the per-family breakdown, or check the Connect IQ Store listing for the live compatibility check at install time.

How are calories calculated?

RuckTrack uses the Pandolf equation, a published military physiology model that estimates metabolic cost from body weight, pack weight, walking speed, grade, and terrain (Road, Gravel, Trail, Sand, Snow). When you slow below walking pace, RuckTrack falls back to your watch's native heart-rate-based estimate so calories don't stall on breaks or steep descents. The total shown is a hybrid of both.

Why are my RuckTrack calories different from the headline number in Garmin Connect?

Two different calculations, two different questions:

  • The big calorie number at the top of an activity in Garmin Connect (the Total Calories line) is Garmin's own heart-rate-based estimate. It doesn't know you were carrying a load. When comparing, use Total, not Active: RuckTrack's numbers include resting burn, so Total is the matching line.
  • RuckTrack's load-aware numbers live under the activity's Stats → Connect IQ section. You'll see fields like Ruck Calories (your total), Calories Without Pack (what the same walk would have cost with no load), and Pack Calorie Bonus (the difference), along with terrain and burn rate.

Both numbers are correct for what they measure. The headline answers "how hard did your body work?" The Connect IQ section answers "how much of that came from the pack?"

The gap can run either way, and it's normal. On a treadmill RuckTrack often reads higher than the headline, because heart-rate calorie models read low when your heart rate is low: a fit walker at an easy pace gets a modest headline number even though the work of the walk is the same, and RuckTrack counts that work. On a flat, easy outdoor walk it can go the other way and read slightly lower. If you want to check it, on a treadmill the speed and incline are exact, and RuckTrack's number lands within a few percent of the standard exercise-physiology walking equation. The Calorie Math page has the worked example, and Does your Garmin count your pack weight? shows the whole thing second by second on a real 45 lb climb.

Why does my pack weight show as "--" in the Garmin Connect activity?

RuckTrack saves your ruck as a Rucking-type activity so Garmin Connect categorizes it correctly (not as a generic hike). That is also why Connect shows a built-in pack-weight field near the top of the activity. Only Garmin's own built-in Rucking app fills that field, and that app ships on a few newer watches (fēnix 8, Forerunner 970, tactix 8). Garmin doesn't let third-party apps like RuckTrack write into it, so on every other watch the field still appears but stays blank ("--").

Your pack weight isn't lost. Open the activity, tap the Stats tab (next to Overview), and scroll to the Connect IQ section, where it's saved as the Pack Weight field alongside the load-aware calories and terrain. That is the reliable place to find it, and where all of RuckTrack's numbers live. RuckTrack also puts the weight in the activity title (for example "Ruck 20 kg"), but Garmin often renames the activity by location (like "Polk County Rucking"), so use the Connect IQ section if the title doesn't show it.

Can I use RuckTrack indoors or on a treadmill?

Yes. There are two indoor modes, selectable from the Settings menu before you start a session:

  • Auto. Uses your watch's heart rate to estimate calories, scaled up by the pack weight you've set. No ongoing input required. Good for varied-incline workouts and instructor-led classes.
  • Manual. Runs the full Pandolf calculation using an incline you enter plus a speed source you choose: the belt speed you type in, or the live pace from a paired foot pod like Stryd. Good for steady-state or interval treadmill walks.

In both indoor modes, GPS is disabled and the elevation stats (vertical speed, elevation gain) are hidden because they aren't meaningful indoors. In Manual, a Pace screen shows your current pace, speed, and incline as you go.

Why does LiveTrack start when I ruck indoors or on a treadmill?

Your watch starts LiveTrack, not RuckTrack, and you can switch it off in one place. In the Garmin Connect app or on the watch, open your LiveTrack settings (under Safety & Tracking) and turn off Auto Start. You can still start LiveTrack by hand any time you do want to share a ruck.

Why it happens: Garmin watches auto-start LiveTrack (the live location-sharing link) for any activity recorded by a Connect IQ app that can use GPS, whether or not GPS is actually running. RuckTrack needs GPS for outdoor rucks, so it carries that permission and the watch treats an indoor session the same way. Connect IQ gives apps no control over LiveTrack, so it can't be switched off for a single session.

Does RuckTrack collect any data?

No. Every setting, preset, and history entry stays on your watch. Meridian operates no servers, runs no analytics, has no companion phone app, and uses no third-party SDKs. Full details on the Privacy page.

Will my history and lifetime totals move to a new watch?

No. RuckTrack's History list and Stats screen (lifetime totals, weekly streak, personal bests) are stored on the watch itself, so a new watch or a fresh reinstall starts those counters from zero. There's no account or cloud copy to restore them from.

Your saved rucks aren't lost, though. Each session syncs to Garmin Connect as a normal activity, with the load-aware fields under Stats → Connect IQ, so your training history lives there regardless of which watch recorded it. It's specifically RuckTrack's on-watch History list and Stats screen that are per-device.

How do the weekly streak and best-pace tiers work?

The Stats screen (Settings → Stats, or the post-save action menu) tracks three things beyond lifetime totals. The weekly streak counts consecutive calendar weeks (Monday start) with at least one saved ruck: one ruck a week keeps it alive, and BEST STREAK records your longest run. THIS WEEK shows how many rucks you've saved in the current week.

Best pace is tracked at three pack-weight tiers (10-19, 20-29, and 30+ lb, or 5-9, 10-14, and 15+ kg, shown in your weight unit). A session needs at least 1 km of distance and a pack at or above the lowest tier to set one; shorter or lighter rucks show "--" for that tier until a qualifying session lands.

If you already have saved rucks, your streak and best paces are built from that history rather than starting at zero.

Can I use a foot pod like Stryd?

Yes. Outdoors there is nothing to set up in RuckTrack at all: it uses whatever speed and distance your watch has already settled on, so if the watch is taking those from your pod, so is RuckTrack. Pair the pod in the watch's sensor settings and set Speed and Distance to Always. Treadmill Auto mode works the same way.

Treadmill Manual mode is the one place you choose, because there you have typed in a belt speed and RuckTrack needs to know which number to believe. Setting up Manual asks for a speed source, Belt Speed or Foot Pod, and you can switch it later from Settings. Foot Pod is off by default. Turn it on and your live pace drives distance and the calorie math, which is what you want for intervals where the belt speed keeps changing. If the pod drops out or reads zero, RuckTrack falls back to your typed belt speed rather than stalling.

The one thing that does not work is running the pod's own Connect IQ data field inside RuckTrack, Stryd's power field for example. No Connect IQ app can host another app's data field. Using the pod as a speed and distance sensor is unaffected and works in every mode. See the screens and data fields question for the workaround.

How do I change pack weight mid-activity?

From the live screen, long-press UP to open the activity Settings menu without pausing. (On touch-only watches without a reliable long-press, hit the Action button to pause and pick Settings from the pause menu.) Tap Pack, set the new weight, and confirm. The new weight counts from that moment on. Calories already banked stay as they were, at the old weight.

Why does a metric show "--" early in an activity?

RuckTrack shows "--" instead of zero whenever a value isn't trustworthy yet. Common cases:

  • Pace shows "--" until your speed is above 0.2 m/s and a GPS fix is available.
  • Vertical Speed shows "--" for the first ~30 seconds while a rolling average warms up, and whenever altitude data is missing or appears to spike.
  • Calories show "--" for the first few seconds while RuckTrack gets a usable speed and heart rate reading.

Nothing is broken and nothing is lost. The dashes clear on their own in the first minute, and distance, time, and calories are being recorded the whole time.

Can I add navigation, reorder the screens, or use another data field (like Stryd)?

No. RuckTrack is a Connect IQ Device App, so it runs as its own self-contained activity with custom-drawn screens (the HR zone arc, the pack-calorie breakdown, the post-ruck summary pages). Garmin's platform doesn't let a Device App add native navigation, reorder its screens the way a native activity profile can, or host other Connect IQ data fields (Stryd's power field, for example).

On Stryd specifically: the pod itself does work with RuckTrack as a speed and distance sensor, on every mode. It is only Stryd's own data field, the one showing running power, that cannot run inside a Device App. See Can I use a foot pod like Stryd? for how to set the pod up.

If you want RuckTrack's load-aware calories alongside any of those, the answer is PackCal, our companion data field. It packages the same Pandolf load-aware calorie model as a data field you add to a native walk-type activity (Ruck, Walk, or Hike), so it runs together with Garmin's native navigation and any other data fields on that activity. PackCal is a separate app on the Connect IQ Store, available on supported watches.

Are all screens available on every watch?

Yes, with one exception. On Instinct 3 Solar and Instinct E, RuckTrack leaves out four review screens: lifetime Stats, History, Calorie Details, and the treadmill Pace page. Everything that records a ruck works normally, and every session still syncs to Garmin Connect, where your full history and per-session stats live anyway. These two watches have far less memory for apps than the rest of the fleet, so the tracking screens get the space. Every other watch, including the Instinct 3 AMOLED, has all screens.

The app says it installed, but the button flips back to "Install". What now?

This is a known Connect IQ problem on Garmin's side rather than something specific to RuckTrack. Garmin has a support article for it and has acknowledged it on several watches, so if you are hitting it you are not doing anything wrong. Your purchase stays on your Garmin account throughout, and none of the steps below charge you again.

Work through these in order, quickest first. The first step has fixed this exact symptom for someone whose phone kept reporting a successful install while nothing ever reached the watch.

  1. Restart the watch, then open the Connect IQ app and sync again. It takes under a minute. If restarting the watch alone does not do it, restart the phone too.
  2. Remove and reinstall from the Connect IQ app on your phone, and let it finish syncing to the watch. Clearing the cache on its own is usually not enough. Worth doing after a restart rather than before, since on its own it can fail the same way many times over.
  3. Check for a pending watch software update and let it finish. A queued firmware update can block Connect IQ transfers over Bluetooth until it completes.
  4. On Android, check the Connect IQ app's permissions and grant it storage, contacts and location. This is the single most reported fix. Note it is the Connect IQ app, not Garmin Connect: they are separate apps with separate permissions. There is no equivalent on iPhone.
  5. Check free space and app slots on the watch itself, not the phone. Every watch has a limit on how many Connect IQ apps, data fields and watch faces it will hold, and when it is full an install can succeed on the phone and never land.
  6. Try Garmin Express on a Windows or Mac computer. The phone app is the easier route and worth trying first, but when it has failed repeatedly Express is the fallback people most often report success with.

If none of that works, get in touch and tell me whether you are on iPhone or Android, and what the watch does when the install finishes: whether anything appears at all, or nothing happens on the watch.

How do I request a refund?

Your purchase goes through Garmin, not through Meridian, so returns are handled on Garmin's side. Meridian doesn't process payments and has no access to your purchase, so I can't reverse a charge or check its status for you.

Contact Garmin Support through the Connect IQ Store with your purchase details. They can tell you what's available for your account and region.
