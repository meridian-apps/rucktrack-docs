Features

For RuckTrack v2.1.0

Starting a ruck

The start screen shows your pack weight, GPS quality, time, battery, and heart rate. Hit START and you're recording. No menu trees, no GPS-lock gate.

QuickLoad lets you pick from saved weight presets or enter a one-time custom load. Presets remember their own unit (lb or kg), so you can keep "Daily 30 lb" and "Long ruck 20 kg" side by side without picking a global unit first. Repeat sessions are one tap.

The six live screens

All six screens are hand-drawn for AMOLED contrast. No squinting at faint gray-on-gray fields. Swipe or use UP/DOWN to move between pages.

  1. Primary — distance (hero), time and total calories below, and a live HR zone arc gauge along the bottom.
  2. Effort — current pace (hero), elevation gain and vertical speed with up/down arrows, and overall average pace.
  3. Calories — total burn (hero), average burn rate and pack bonus percentage, plus a bottom line showing the pack, terrain, and any kcal earned over an unloaded baseline.
  4. Lap — current lap pace (hero), lap distance and lap time, and the overall elapsed timer.
  5. HR zones — current heart rate (hero), zone label, and a circular zone gauge with a pulsating heart icon.
  6. Clock — current time with seconds, today's date, and a battery indicator that turns red when low.

Treadmill modes drop the Effort page because vertical speed and elevation aren't meaningful indoors. Five pages in treadmill, six outdoors.

Load-aware calories

This is the wedge. Generic profiles like Walk and Hike don't know your pack weight, so they under-credit a loaded carry by 10 to 20 percent per the published research. RuckTrack uses the Pandolf load-carriage equation to compute calories from body weight, pack weight, walking speed, terrain grade, and surface type. The numbers reflect what your body actually did.

Four terrain coefficients: Road (1.00), Gravel (1.05), Trail (1.20), Sand (1.50). Pick one before you start, or change mid-activity. The terrain at save time is what writes to the FIT file.

When you slow below walking pace or the GPS goes flaky, RuckTrack falls back to the watch's heart-rate-based estimate so calories don't stall on breaks or steep descents. The total is a hybrid of both.

See the calorie math explainer for the equation, the Santee descent correction, and the hybrid fallback logic.

Indoor and treadmill modes

Treadmill mode handles the case where GPS won't help you and Garmin's HR-only profiles ignore your pack. Two indoor recording modes, picked at the start:

  • Auto. Uses your watch's heart-rate-based calorie estimate, scaled up by the pack weight you've set. No incline or belt-speed input required. Good for varied-incline workouts and instructor-led classes where the machine settings change constantly.
  • Manual. Runs the full Pandolf calculation using an incline (0 to 15 percent) and belt speed (mph or km/h) you enter. Good for steady-state treadmill walks where you can read the belt speed off the machine.

In both indoor modes, GPS is disabled and the Effort page is hidden. Sessions save as Indoor Walk in Garmin Connect (Garmin's only indoor-walk subsport), and the pack-aware fields write the same way as outdoor sessions.

Mid-activity controls

Pack weight, terrain, incline, and belt speed can all change during a session without pausing the timer. Long-press UP on any live screen to open the Settings menu, pick the value, confirm. The Pandolf model picks up the new values on the next 1-second tick. Past calorie integration is not retroactively recomputed.

On touch-only watches without a reliable long-press, hit the Action button to pause and pick Settings from the pause menu. The pause menu also has a Lap option for touch-only devices.

Auto Lap and manual laps

Auto Lap fires a lap marker every 1 mile (or 1 km, based on your distance unit). Each lap triggers a short haptic and a 3-second lap-summary view showing the lap's distance, time, and pace, then returns to the live screen. Manual laps fire the same haptic and summary on the Back button.

If you record a manual lap before the next auto-lap marker, the auto-lap counter advances one full unit from that point. You don't get a double-lap at the next mile mark.

Auto Lap is on by default. Toggle it in Settings, before or during an activity.

Sound cues

Short tones fire at start, pause, resume, save, and discard. Same audio rhythm as Garmin's built-in activity profiles, so the app feels native to the recording flow you already know. Silent on watches without a speaker.

Post-save summary

A six-page summary opens automatically after you save. Swipe or use UP/DOWN to move through it:

  1. Overview — distance hero, time and average pace, pack weight. An adaptive title like "Nice Climb" or "Strong Finish" reflects what the session actually was.
  2. Calories — total calories, average burn rate, pack bonus, and a two-line footer with the kcal and percent earned from your pack.
  3. Elevation — filled elevation chart, total ascent and descent. (Outdoor only — treadmill modes skip this page.)
  4. HR & zones — zone histogram, time per zone, average and max HR.
  5. Lifetime totals — your cumulative ruck count, total distance, total time, and total calories carried. Updates to include the session you just saved, so the numbers climb every time.
  6. Stats Guide — pointer to Garmin Connect's Stats → Connect IQ section, where the load-aware fields live. Discoverability matters; this page closes the loop.

Press the Action button on any summary page to open Calorie Details — a scrolling list of every input that fed the math: total burn, calories without pack, pack bonus in kcal and percent, your watch's native estimate, plus pack weight, terrain, body weight, and the speed and grade ranges used.

In Garmin Connect

Outdoor sessions classify as Rucking (Garmin's FIT subsport 124, fleet-wide). Activity names auto-populate as "Ruck 35 lb" or "Treadmill Ruck 16 kg" so the activity list reads at a glance — no manual rename.

The activity's Stats → Connect IQ section shows seven load-aware fields:

  • Pack Weight (lb or kg, in the unit you carried)
  • Ruck Calories — your total, Pandolf + native fallback
  • Calories Without Pack — the same session with no load, for comparison
  • Pack Calorie Bonus — the kcal earned from carrying weight
  • Burn Rate — average kcal per hour
  • Terrain Surface — Road, Gravel, Trail, or Sand
  • Treadmill Mode and Incline (where applicable)

Per-second grade and metabolic rate write to the FIT record stream, so Garmin Connect renders them as time-series charts on the activity detail page alongside the native pace, heart-rate, and elevation graphs.

Widget loop glance

Scroll to RuckTrack in your watch's widget loop to see your last saved session: distance, duration, pack weight, and calories. One-glance check; no need to open the app.

History view

From the start-screen Settings menu, pick History to see your last 10 saved rucks: date, distance, duration, pack weight, and calories per card. Newest first. Read-only — for editing or deleting an activity, use Garmin Connect.

Privacy and data

Every setting, preset, and history entry stays on your watch. Meridian operates no servers, runs no analytics, has no companion phone app, and uses no third-party SDKs. The full privacy policy covers what gets stored locally and how Garmin Connect handles the FIT file.
